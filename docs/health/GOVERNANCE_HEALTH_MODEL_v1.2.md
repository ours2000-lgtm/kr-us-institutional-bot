Canonical Governance Health Constitution

Supersedes: v1.1

1. PURPOSE

The Governance Health Model defines the adaptive framework for evaluating governance performance, resilience, stability, and risk exposure across all governance layers.

This version introduces dynamic evaluation, root-cause analytics, and stability indices to enhance predictive governance capabilities.

2. CORE OBJECTIVES

continuous health monitoring
risk visibility
adaptive evaluation
stability assessment
predictive analytics
closed-loop improvement

3. CORE PRINCIPLES

Health evaluation MUST remain evidence-based.

Dynamic thresholds SHOULD reflect operational realities.

Health metrics MUST support explainability.

Simulation feedback MUST influence health assessment.

Governance health MUST enable proactive decision-making.

4. HEALTH DIMENSIONS

(EFFECTIVENESS, RESPONSIVENESS, RESILIENCE, COVERAGE, CONSISTENCY, TRANSPARENCY, MATURITY unchanged from v1.1)

5. DYNAMIC THRESHOLDS

Health evaluations MAY use adaptive thresholds instead of fixed limits.

Examples include:

rolling averages (e.g., 90-day baseline)
seasonality adjustments
workload-aware thresholds

Threshold models MUST record:

threshold_model_id
baseline_window
policy_reference

Threshold changes MUST be recorded as evidence.

6. COMPOSITE GOVERNANCE STABILITY INDEX

A Governance Stability Index SHALL be calculated using:

governance_health_score
risk_exposure_score
consistency_violation_rate

This index represents the overall operational stability posture.

7. RISK-HEALTH INDEX

(unchanged — combined risk + health visibility)

8. HEALTH STATES

HEALTHY
DEGRADED
CRITICAL

9. ROOT CAUSE ANALYTICS

When health degradation is detected, the system SHOULD perform automated root-cause analysis.

Possible root factors include:

control failures
rule coverage gaps
policy violations
dependency instability
assurance drift

Root-cause findings MUST be recorded as governance evidence.

10. PREDICTIVE HEALTH ANALYTICS

(unchanged — predictive modelling retained)

11. CROSS-DIMENSION CORRELATION

(unchanged — correlation analysis retained)

12. CONTINUOUS SIMULATION FEEDBACK LOOP

Simulation and chaos test outcomes SHALL directly influence health metrics.

Closed-loop validation cycle:

simulate → measure → improve → re-test

Simulation outcomes MUST update:

resilience indicators
maturity scores
stability index

13. SIGNAL INTEGRATION

Health Model SHALL ingest signals from:

Validation Engine
Assurance Engine
Incident Management
Dependency Matrix
Control Library

14. STAKEHOLDER-SPECIFIC HEALTH VIEWS

Health reporting SHOULD provide tailored views for different stakeholders.

Examples:

Regulators → transparency and compliance metrics

Executives → resilience and risk exposure

Operations → responsiveness and incident metrics

Auditors → consistency and evidence integrity

Views SHALL follow disclosure policy.

15. BENCHMARK ALIGNMENT

Benchmark targets SHALL remain defined by policy.

Deviation MUST generate governance signals.

16. IMPROVEMENT TRIGGERS

Health degradation MUST trigger improvement actions with evidence tracking.

17. TRANSPARENCY REPORTING

Reports SHALL include:

dimension scores
weights
stability index
root-cause insights
trend indicators

18. AUTOMATION

Health evaluation SHOULD be automated and continuously updated.

19. DATA SOURCES

Telemetry
Assurance outputs
Incidents
Benchmarks
Simulations

20. RESILIENCE OF HEALTH MODEL

The model MUST remain operational during degraded system states.

The Governance Health Model SHALL serve as the adaptive intelligence layer for governance performance monitoring, stability assessment, and predictive risk awareness.
